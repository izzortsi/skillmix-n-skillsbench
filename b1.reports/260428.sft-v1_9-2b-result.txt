on Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B with LoRA r=16, alpha=32) lifted the post-SFT student
above its baseline AND above pre-SFT haiku reference:

                              BL pass    CU pass    Δ
    Pre-SFT  qwen3.5:0.8b     0.510      0.565      +0.055
    Pre-SFT  haiku-4-5  (ref) 0.785      0.800      +0.015
    Post-SFT v1   (0.8B LoRA) 0.635      0.585      -0.050
    Post-SFT v1.9 (2B  LoRA)  0.750      0.825      +0.075

DELTAS

    v1.9 vs pre-SFT 0.8B:     ΔBL +0.240   ΔCU +0.260
    v1.9 vs post-SFT v1:      ΔBL +0.115   ΔCU +0.240
    v1.9 vs pre-SFT haiku:    ΔBL -0.035   ΔCU +0.025

ATTRIBUTION SPLIT (added 2026-05-06 with pre-SFT 2B control)

    Pre-SFT 2B (Qwen3.5-2B base, no SFT, same SOLVER prompt) was
    benched against the same 200-task holdout to attribute v1.9's
    lift between base-model scaling and SFT contribution:

                                BL pass    CU pass    Δ
        Pre-SFT 0.8B            0.510      0.565      +0.055
        Pre-SFT 2B   (NEW)      0.685      0.710      +0.025
        v1.9 (2B+LoRA)          0.750      0.825      +0.075

    Base scaling 0.8B → 2B:        ΔBL +0.175   ΔCU +0.145
    SFT contribution at 2B:         ΔBL +0.065   ΔCU +0.115
    SFT differential lift (Δ):      pre-SFT 2B Δ = +0.025
                                    v1.9 Δ        = +0.075
                                    SFT Δ-lift    = +0.050

    The original v1.9 framing ("model size unlocks the ceiling")
    overstated the SFT contribution by attributing the full v1.9 - v1
    gap (+24 pp CU) to model size. With the proper pre-SFT 2B control,
    the split is: +14.5 pp from base scaling alone, +11.5 pp from SFT
    on top of base 2B. SFT does substantial work — it just looked like
    base scaling because the comparison was against pre-SFT 0.8B.

    Bench-eval-pre-sft-4b ran into a format-compliance artifact: base
    Qwen3.5-4B reasons correctly but doesn't reliably emit literal
    "ANSWER:" lines, so the deterministic judge scores it ~0/200. The
    v2.0 attribution split is therefore bounded but not measurable on
    this bench. See b1.reports/260506.bench-format-sensitivity-
    finding.txt.

================================================================================
WHAT THIS OVERTURNS
================================================================================

The 260426 findings report concluded that three well-formed 0.8B recipes
(v1, v1.6, v1.8) clustered CU into a 2 pp band of 0.565-0.585, and inferred
"the ceiling sits somewhere model-internal, not in the training recipe."
v1.9 confirms that diagnosis: at 2B, the SAME recipe lifts CU by +24 pp
over the best 0.8B recipe.

Specifically:

  - The 0.8B "ceiling" was a model-capacity ceiling, not a corpus or
    objective ceiling. Adding contrastive rows (v1.5), dropping ceiling
    skills (v1.6), going to partial FT (v1.7), or stripping the skill
    frame (v1.8) all produced CU within 2 pp of v1's 0.585. None could
    cross that band because 0.8B did not have the capacity to use the
    procedure differentially while ALSO maintaining basic format
    competence.

  - At 2B, the same 353 rows is enough to teach BOTH format AND
    differential procedure use. CU 0.825 vs BL 0.750 — the +7.5 pp gap
    means the model is genuinely benefiting from the injected procedure,
    not just learning to produce procedure-shaped responses.

================================================================================
WHAT v1.9 DOES NOT YET TELL US
================================================================================

  1. Whether 1.5B / 1.7B would also clear the gates. We jumped from 0.8B
     to 2B; the smallest-model-that-works is unknown. Cheap to test
     (Qwen3-1.7B is closest available) if curiosity warrants it.

  2. Whether more corpus would push CU higher still. The 2B model at
     353 rows already exceeds haiku-4-5's 0.800 CU reference. Returns to
     scaling the corpus may be small from here; the experiment may have
     reached the natural ceiling of the 200-task / 40-skill holdout.

  3. Whether the per-skill pattern is now uniform-positive or still
     bimodal (lift on weak skills, regression on ceiling skills, like v1
     showed at n=77). Per-skill diff for v1.9 vs pre-SFT-2B-baseline
     would settle this. Worth running if any decision hinges on which
     skills the model now uses procedurally.

================================================================================
NEXT-AXIS OPTIONS
================================================================================

The original experiment hypothesis is now confirmed. From here the
question becomes "what's next on top of v1.9", not "is the hypothesis
right". Three directions, in increasing investment:

A. SHIP-IT (no further work)
   The experiment closes with v1.9 as the final result. Update the README
   to call the SFT pipeline validated, archive v1.5/v1.6/v1.7/v1.8 as
   negative-result artifacts, and move on.

B. PER-SKILL DIAGNOSTIC (cheap, ~30 min)
   Re-run the v1.9 eval through training/compare_pre_post.py against a
   fresh pre-SFT-2B baseline (same 200 tasks, no SFT). Establishes:
     - Per-skill lift / flat / regression cluster structure at 2B
     - Whether the bimodal pattern from v1 still appears at 2B
     - Whether any skills hit a 100% ceiling (would inform corpus
       trimming for v1.10)
   Recommended only if planning further iterations.

C. SMALLEST-MODEL-THAT-WORKS (cheap, ~1 GPU-hour)
   Repeat v1.9 recipe on Qwen3-1.7B. Closes the model-size envelope —
   the headline becomes "X B params + 353 rows of curated demos is
   enough to learn differential procedural use", where X is the smaller
   of {1.7B, 2B}.

D. CORPUS SCALING (medium, ~$50-100 + 4-8 GPU-hours)
   Generate 10x rows by widening the task pool (e.g., 40 skills × 50
   tasks/skill = 2000 tasks → ~1500 curated-passing rows). Re-run v1
   recipe on 0.8B with the larger corpus to test whether corpus scale
   alone can also break the 0.585 0.8B ceiling. Diagnostic: separates
   "model capacity" from "corpus information content" as causal axes
   for the 0.8B failure.

E. DPO / REJECTION-SAMPLING (largest, ~$100-200)
   Take v1.9 as the SFT-warmstart and run DPO on (CU-passing, CU-failing)
   pairs from the holdout-like distribution. Tests whether even higher
   CU is reachable with a preference-learning objective.

RECOMMENDATION

   B (per-skill diagnostic) as a near-free check; then choose between A
   (close the experiment), C (size envelope), or D (corpus scale).
   Defer E unless v1.9's ceiling becomes the new bottleneck.
