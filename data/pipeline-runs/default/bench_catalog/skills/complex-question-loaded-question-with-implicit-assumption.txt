# complex-question-loaded-question-with-implicit-assumption

A question that is loaded with an implicit assumption.

## When to Use

- Apply when asked to identify, analyze, or flag questions whose phrasing forces the answerer to accept an unstated or disputed claim.

## Procedure

1. Locate every interrogative sentence in the passage or challenge.
2. For each question, list the propositions that must already be true for the question to make sense (its presuppositions).
3. Test each presupposition by asking whether a direct answer ('yes' or explanation) would force the respondent to concede that proposition.
4. Check whether the presupposition is contested, unestablished, or unfavorable to the respondent rather than mutually accepted background.
5. Mark the question as carrying an implicit assumption only if at least one presupposition is both non-trivial and not conceded elsewhere in the passage.
6. Record which specific proposition is being smuggled in, distinct from the surface question being asked.

## Constraints

- Do not flag a question merely because it is pointed, accusatory, or emotionally charged if its presuppositions are already established facts.
- Do not flag open-ended questions (who/what/why) that have no hidden claim beyond the topic itself.
- Require that a direct answer logically entails agreement with the contested proposition; mere topical relevance is not enough.
- Do not conflate a factual error inside a question with an embedded presupposition smuggled past the answerer.

## Example

Positive: A question asks 'When did you stop cheating on the exams?' in a context where cheating has not been established. Why: Any dated answer concedes prior cheating, which was never agreed to.
Negative: A question asks 'Why did the suspect confess?' in a passage that has already stated the suspect confessed. Why: The presupposition is an established fact in the passage, not a smuggled assumption.
