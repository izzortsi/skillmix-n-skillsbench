REPORT: STATISTICAL SIGNIFICANCE OF v1.9 / v2.0 Δ ESTIMATES

Date:   2026-05-06
Status: COMPLETED — supersedes the verbal "within noise" framing in
        260428.sft-v2_0-4b-result.txt and the paper draft.

================================================================================
QUESTION
================================================================================

The v1.9 and v2.0 result reports state Δ = pass_rate(curated) -
pass_rate(baseline) as +0.075 and +0.045 respectively, and characterize
the v1.9 → v2.0 shrinkage as evidence of bench saturation. The paper
draft hedges with "within one standard error of each other". This
report runs the actual tests:

    1. Per-model McNemar's test on the paired (BL, CU) verdicts —
       is Δ significantly different from zero?
    2. Per-model bootstrap 95% CI on Δ — point estimate + uncertainty.
    3. Cross-model bootstrap test — is v1.9's Δ significantly larger
       than v2.0's? (the bench-saturation claim)

Implementation: training/significance_tests.py. McNemar uses continuity
correction for n_discordant >= 25, exact binomial otherwise. Bootstrap
uses 10,000 resamples of task_uids with replacement; cross-model test
resamples each model independently (correct under the assumption that
v1.9 and v2.0 are independent eval runs on the same task list).

================================================================================
RESULTS
================================================================================

v1.9 (Qwen3.5-2B + LoRA r=16)
    contingency: a=140  b(BL only)=10  c(CU only)=25  d(both fail)=25
    Δ = +0.0750
    McNemar two-sided χ²=5.60, p=0.018          ← significant at α=0.05
    bootstrap 95% CI on Δ: [+0.020, +0.130]
    => v1.9's positive Δ is statistically real.

v2.0 (Qwen3.5-4B + LoRA r=32)
    contingency: a=163  b(BL only)=4  c(CU only)=13  d(both fail)=20
    Δ = +0.0450
    McNemar two-sided exact-binomial p=0.049    ← borderline at α=0.05
    bootstrap 95% CI on Δ: [+0.005, +0.085]
    => v2.0's positive Δ is barely distinguishable from zero;
       under Bonferroni correction for 2 tests it would not survive.

v1.9 vs v2.0 (the bench-saturation test)
    Δ_v1.9 - Δ_v2.0 = +0.0300
    bootstrap 95% CI on (Δ_v1.9 - Δ_v2.0): [-0.040, +0.100]
    one-sided bootstrap p (H₀: Δ_v1.9 ≤ Δ_v2.0): p = 0.225
    => Cannot reject the null. The v1.9 → v2.0 shrinkage of Δ
       (+0.030 in absolute terms) is NOT statistically distinguishable
       from sampling noise at n=200 per condition.

================================================================================
INTERPRETATION — WHAT WE CAN AND CANNOT CLAIM
================================================================================

CLAIMS THAT SURVIVE THE TEST:

  1. SFT teaches differential procedural skill use at 2B.
     v1.9's +0.075 Δ is statistically real (p=0.018, CI excludes 0).
     This supports the original SFT hypothesis.

  2. SFT contributes a small but real procedural lift at 4B.
     v2.0's +0.045 Δ is borderline-significant on its own (p=0.049,
     CI = [+0.005, +0.085]). The 4B model does benefit from skill
     injection, just by a smaller absolute margin than the 2B model.

  3. Curated and baseline pass rates separately are well-distinguished
     in both v1.9 and v2.0. The absolute lifts (e.g. v2.0 BL 0.835 vs
     v1.9 BL 0.750) easily clear binomial noise at n=200.

CLAIMS THAT DO NOT SURVIVE THE TEST:

  1. "v2.0's Δ is smaller than v1.9's, indicating bench saturation."
     Directionally true (+0.030 absolute drop) but p=0.225, 95% CI
     on the difference includes zero. At n=200 we cannot statistically
     distinguish saturation from noise.

  2. Any model-size-conditional comparison of Δ values that relies on
     v1.9 vs v2.0 differences. The direction of the effect is
     consistent with saturation, but the magnitude is within sampling
     uncertainty.

CONFIRMING SATURATION WOULD REQUIRE ONE OF:

  A. Larger n. To detect a Δ difference of ~0.030 at α=0.05, power 0.80,
     paired binary outcomes, we estimate n ≈ 800–1200 per condition.
     Cost: ~$50–100 in additional eval and judge calls (4–6× current).

  B. Harder bench. A compose-skill or adversarial bench where the
     procedure provides 2-3× larger absolute lift would push v1.9's
     Δ to a value far enough from v2.0's that n=200 is sufficient.
     This is the option recommended in the v2.0 result report's
     next-axis section.

  C. Multi-seed eval. Run v2.0 inference at two or three seeds (with
     temperature > 0) and aggregate. Reduces per-condition variance
     without increasing task count. Cost: ~$5–10.
