================================================================================
QUESTION
================================================================================

The 260506 format-sensitivity finding (b1.reports/260506.bench-format-
sensitivity-finding.txt) showed that the deterministic judge (used for
88.5% of holdout tasks) requires literal "ANSWER:" lines, which pre-SFT
base models do not reliably emit. To enable apples-to-apples
attribution comparison between pre-SFT base models (which need LLM
judge to be measured fairly) and post-SFT students, we ran a full
LLM-only re-judge of v1.9 and v2.0 episodes — using the existing
model responses, just routing them all through the LLM judge
regardless of original query_type.

This report documents the resulting score shifts and what they imply
for the experiment's headline claims.

SETUP
================================================================================

Method     training/rejudge_failed_episodes.py with new flags:
             --all                   target all 400 episodes per run
             --force-llm-judge       override query_type to FREE_FORM
             --out-dir <new>         preserve the originals

Judge      anthropic:claude-opus-4-7 (same as original eval)
Inputs     existing episodes.json files from
             bench-eval-post-sft/episodes.json              (v1, 0.8B)

Output     bench-eval-post-sft-v1-llm-only/
           bench-eval-post-sft-v1_9-llm-only/
           bench-eval-post-sft-v2_0-llm-only/
           bench-eval-pre-sft-haiku-llm-only/

Cost       ~$5 per run × 4 runs = ~$20 total. Runs in ~10 min each
           (no model generation; pure judge calls). All 400 episodes
           per run successfully re-judged; zero remaining judge
           errors.

FOUR OBSERVATIONS

  1. BOTH conditions lift under LLM-only judging at ALL FOUR students.
     The deterministic judge was under-counting passes universally;
     even SFT'd models trained to produce ANSWER: format sometimes
     produce conclusions in alternative phrasings that the extractor
     missed.

  2. Curated lifts MORE than baseline under re-judge for the THREE
     SFT'd students (ΔCU > ΔBL by +2-4.5 pp). Procedure-following
     responses bury conclusions in procedural narration more often
     than direct-thinking responses. The deterministic judge was
     systematically biased AGAINST the curated condition, under-
     stating SFT's contribution. (Haiku has the opposite tiny
     direction, ΔΔ +0.015 in BL's favor — within noise; haiku's
     responses are uniformly polished regardless of condition.)

  3. v2.0 CU is now 0.985 (197/200) — TIED with the haiku reference
     CU at 0.985 (197/200). The "v2.0 exceeds the haiku reference"
     framing in the original v2.0 report becomes "v2.0 matches the
     haiku reference at curated ceiling" under fair scoring.
     Both students sit at the bench's effective ceiling.

  4. v1's (0.8B SFT) Δ was NOT actually negative — the original
     -0.050 was a deterministic-judge artifact. Under fair scoring,
     v1's Δ is -0.005 (essentially zero). The sharper "format-only
     learning" claim survives: 0.8B SFT lifts BL by +20 pp over
     pre-SFT but lifts CU by only +14 pp, producing zero
     differential procedural application. Not a regression, just
     no SFT-attributable Δ at this model scale.

THE BASE-MODEL PROCEDURAL RESPONSIVENESS IS W-SHAPED

   Pre-SFT Δ across capability tier (HF + LLM-only):
      -0.075 (0.8B) → +0.060 (2B) → -0.010 (4B) → +0.030 (haiku)

   Two negative-Δ pockets at 0.8B and 4B; positive at 2B and haiku.
   The procedure injection helps or hurts a base depending on the
   regime. Likely mechanisms differ by pocket:
      - At 0.8B: base lacks the capacity to follow the 5-step
        procedure correctly. Errors compound at every step; the
        procedure adds compounding rather than scaffolding.
      - At 4B: base is competent enough to solve baseline tasks
        directly without the procedure, so the 5-step procedure
        adds overhead without adding signal --- the procedure
        introduces sub-inference error
        opportunities on tasks the base can one-shot.
      - Base 4B may over-defer to the procedure's framing on tasks
        where the procedure is mildly mismatched.
   We do not have enough single-skill data to disambiguate.

THE SFT CONTRIBUTION TO Δ IS ROUGHLY UNIFORM ACROSS SIZES (2026-05-09)

   With pre-SFT 0.8B HF/LLM-only now in hand (BL 0.665 / CU 0.590 /
   Δ -0.075), the SFT-attributable Δ-lift is measurable at all three
   model sizes under matched-path scoring:

       Student / SFT recipe       pre-SFT Δ → post-SFT Δ    SFT Δ-lift
       0.8B (LoRA r=8,  v1)       -0.075 → -0.005           +0.070
       2B   (LoRA r=16, v1.9)     +0.060 → +0.100           +0.040
       4B   (LoRA r=32, v2.0)     -0.010 → +0.065           +0.075

   The Δ-lift sits in a 4 pp band (+0.040 to +0.075) across an
   ORDER OF MAGNITUDE in base-model capacity. The bench is saturated
   in ABSOLUTE pass rates (v2.0 ties haiku at 0.985 CU), but the SFT
   mechanism delivers comparable differential procedural lift at all
   three sizes.

   Two earlier framings of this work are now path-mismatch artifacts:
      1. v1's "format-only learning at 0.8B" diagnosis was based on
         comparing v1 Δ -0.005 against pre-SFT 0.8B Ollama Δ +0.055,
         suggesting SFT had failed at 0.8B. Under matched scoring,
         v1's SFT Δ-lift is +0.070, comparable to 4B's +0.075.
      2. The previous version of this report claimed "the SFT
         contribution at 4B is the LARGEST" or "monotonically
         increasing 2B → 4B." With pre-SFT 0.8B HF/LLM-only in
         hand, 4B's +0.075 Δ-lift is on par with 0.8B's +0.070,
         not uniquely large. The accurate framing is mechanism-
         uniformity rather than capacity-conditional amplification
         (or saturation).

REGIME-ASYMMETRIC LIFT (2026-05-09 OBSERVATION)

   The roughly capacity-invariant Δ-lift is doing visibly different
   work at different scales:

       0.8B:  pre-SFT Δ -0.075 → post-SFT Δ -0.005 (+0.070 lift)
              ===> rescues procedure-incompetent regime to neutral
       2B:    pre-SFT Δ +0.060 → post-SFT Δ +0.100 (+0.040 lift)
              ===> marginally improves procedure-friendly regime
       4B:    pre-SFT Δ -0.010 → post-SFT Δ +0.065 (+0.075 lift)
              ===> flips procedure-overhead to procedure-helps

   SFT works harder (in absolute Δ-lift terms) where the base
   STRUGGLES with the procedure; it does less work where the base
   is already in a procedure-friendly regime. This is consistent
   with the W-shape mechanism explanation: at 0.8B and 4B, the base
   struggles for different reasons (capacity vs competence-overhead),
   and SFT teaches the model to overcome those obstacles. At 2B,
   the base is already in the sweet spot, so SFT has less headroom.

   Falsifiable prediction at 8B/14B: the procedure should hurt the
   base less than at 4B (since the base can use it natively, like
   haiku), and SFT's Δ-lift should shrink toward 2B-ish levels.

THE ABSOLUTE SFT CONTRIBUTION ALSO TRACKS THE Δ-LIFT BAND

   SFT contribution to absolute pass rates (LLM-only):

       Student      ΔBL (SFT)    ΔCU (SFT)    Δ-lift
       0.8B (v1)    +0.045       +0.115       +0.070
       2B (v1.9)    +0.060       +0.100       +0.040
       4B (v2.0)    +0.070       +0.145       +0.075

   The CU contribution at 4B (+0.145) IS the largest in absolute
   terms. The differential Δ-lift, however, is on par with 0.8B and
   well within the 4 pp band that holds across all three sizes.

UPDATED INTERPRETATION OF "SATURATION"

   What's actually happening at 4B:
      - Base 4B already performs well on baseline (0.850 BL)
      - Base 4B does NOT use procedure injection well (Δ -0.010)
      - SFT teaches the model to USE the procedure productively,
        flipping Δ from -0.010 to +0.065 and pushing CU to 0.985
      - Most of the SFT lift is in the curated condition, not baseline

   The bench's saturation cap (~0.985 CU at the top) constrains the
   absolute headroom available, but SFT is taking advantage of the
   curated headroom that base 4B is leaving on the table. Far from
   saturated in differential terms.

   The remaining open question is whether this trend continues to
   higher base capability (8B, 14B). Plausibly, the 4B "trough" in
   pre-SFT Δ recovers at higher scales as the base becomes capable
   enough to use the procedure naturally — haiku (frontier) at +0.030
   is already partway back. If true, SFT's 4B-peak is a transient
   regime where the base is capable enough to be benchmarked but not
   capable enough to use procedures natively.

Both Δs are now strongly distinguishable from zero:
  - v1.9: p=0.0027 (was p=0.018 under mixed-judge)
  - v2.0: p=0.00098 (was p=0.049 under mixed-judge)

v2.0's Δ now survives Bonferroni correction across the two tests
comfortably (p_adj ≈ 0.002 < 0.025). The earlier worry that v2.0's
Δ was "barely significant" was an artifact of the deterministic
judge under-counting curated wins.

Cross-model bootstrap (paired bootstrap saturating-direction test):
  Δ_v1.9 - Δ_v2.0 = +0.035
  bootstrap 95% CI on the difference: [-0.040, +0.105]
  one-sided p (H₀: Δ_v1.9 ≤ Δ_v2.0): p = 0.191

Saturation result is essentially unchanged from the deterministic-
judge path:
  - mixed-judge:    Δ_v1.9 - Δ_v2.0 = +0.030, p = 0.225
  - LLM-only:       Δ_v1.9 - Δ_v2.0 = +0.035, p = 0.191

The shrinkage of Δ from 2B to 4B is robust to the scoring method —
both judging paths agree on the magnitude (~+0.030 to +0.035) and
on the conclusion that the shrinkage is directional but not
statistically distinguishable at n=200.

================================================================================
WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE PAPER
================================================================================

POSITIVE RESULTS THAT STRENGTHEN

  1. The SFT effect at 2B is more robustly significant than the
     paper currently states. Update v1.9 reporting: Δ +0.100, p=0.003.

  2. The SFT effect at 4B is also robustly significant (no longer
     borderline, no longer Bonferroni-fragile). Update v2.0
     reporting: Δ +0.065, p<0.001.

  3. v2.0 CU 0.985 ties the haiku-4-5 reference CU at 0.985 (197/200
     identical pass count). The original "v2.0 exceeds haiku
     reference" claim becomes "v2.0 matches haiku at curated
     ceiling." This is a stronger statement: the bench is
     comprehensively saturated at the top under fair scoring,
     supported by the absolute pass-rate ceiling rather than just
     the Δ shrinkage trend.

CLAIMS THAT ARE UNCHANGED

  4. Bench-saturation cross-model test (v1.9 vs v2.0 only) still
     fails on the post-SFT Δ values (p=0.191 one-sided, CI includes
     0). The TYPE of saturation has been re-characterised by the
     pre-SFT 4B finding (see CROSS-STUDENT Δ TREND above): the
     bench is saturated in absolute pass rates but the SFT
     contribution to Δ is monotonically increasing 2B → 4B.

NEW CLAIMS (POSITIVE)

  5. v2.0 ties the frontier haiku reference at curated ceiling
     (CU 0.985 each, identical pass count 197/200). Reframe the
     v2.0 result from "exceeds haiku" to "matches haiku."

  6. v1's "Δ flipped negative" finding was a deterministic-judge
     artifact. Under fair scoring, v1 Δ is essentially zero
     (-0.005). The "format-only learning" claim sharpens: 0.8B
     SFT lifts BL by +20 pp over the original pre-SFT but lifts
     CU by only +14 pp, producing zero differential procedural
     application. The capacity-floor diagnosis at 0.8B holds.

  7. ΔCU > ΔBL on re-judge across all three SFT'd students means
     the deterministic judge was systematically biased AGAINST the
     curated condition. Procedure-following responses bury
     conclusions in narration. The bench was systematically
     under-stating SFT's contribution.

  8. (NEW 2026-05-09) PRE-SFT 4B HAS NEGATIVE PROCEDURAL Δ.
     Base 4B: BL 0.850 / CU 0.840 / Δ -0.010. The base model is
     competent enough to solve baseline tasks but does not benefit
     from procedure injection (and is mildly hurt by it). This
     overturns the previous "saturation = SFT contribution shrinks"
     framing. The SFT contribution at 4B is +0.075 Δ-lift —
     NEARLY DOUBLE the +0.040 Δ-lift at 2B. The bench is saturated
     in absolute pass rates but the SFT MECHANISM is doing more
     work, not less, at 4B than at 2B. This is the strongest single
     positive finding for the SFT methodology in the entire
     experiment, and the paper's saturation-and-attribution sections
     need to be rewritten to reflect it.

  9. (NEW 2026-05-09) THE BASE-MODEL PROCEDURAL RESPONSIVENESS IS
     NON-MONOTONIC. Pre-SFT Δ across capability tier traces
     0.055 (0.8B) → 0.060 (2B) → -0.010 (4B) → +0.030 (haiku).
     The 4B "trough" is the most distinctive feature of the curve.
     Possible mechanism: 4B is in a regime where the base is
     capable enough to make procedure injection counterproductive
     (over-thinks; sub-inference errors) but not capable enough to
     use procedures natively. Frontier students like haiku recover
     partway. SFT's value may be transient to this 4B-class regime.
