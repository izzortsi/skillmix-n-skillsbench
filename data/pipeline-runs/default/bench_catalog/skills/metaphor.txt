# metaphor

a figure of speech that, for rhetorical effect, directly refers to one thing by mentioning another.

## When to Use

- When asked to identify, interpret, or construct an implicit comparison where one thing is described as being another unlike thing without 'like' or 'as'.

## Procedure

1. Scan the passage for sentences that equate or substitute one noun/concept for another unlike thing.
2. For each candidate, confirm the comparison is direct (uses 'is', 'are', 'becomes', or renaming) rather than hedged with 'like' or 'as'.
3. Check that the two things compared belong to different categories or domains, so the statement is not literally true.
4. Infer the shared attribute the author wants transferred from the vehicle to the tenor (role, quality, function).
5. Match that inference to the challenge's question (identify, interpret, or apply) and select or construct the response accordingly.
6. Reject candidates whose comparison is literal, idiomatic-but-dead, or uses explicit comparison markers.

## Constraints

- Do not flag comparisons that use explicit markers such as 'like', 'as', 'resembles', or 'similar to'.
- Do not flag statements that are literally true even if vivid; the two terms must belong to distinct categories.
- Require a non-literal equation of two entities; mere descriptive adjectives or personification alone do not qualify.
- Do not treat fixed idioms whose figurative sense has faded as instances of this device.

## Example

Positive: A passage states 'Her voice was a warm blanket on a cold night' when discussing how a speaker comforted listeners. Why: A voice (auditory) is directly equated with a blanket (tactile object) to transfer the quality of comfort without 'like' or 'as'.
Negative: A passage states 'Her voice was like a warm blanket on a cold night.' Why: The explicit comparison marker 'like' makes this a simile rather than a direct substitution of one thing for another.
