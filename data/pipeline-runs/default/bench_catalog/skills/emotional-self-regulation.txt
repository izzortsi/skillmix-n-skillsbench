# emotional-self-regulation

a complex process that involves initiating, inhibiting, or modulating one's state or behavior in a given situation.

## When to Use

- when asked to detect or identify instances where a character consciously manages their own emotional state or behavioral response to a triggering situation.

## Procedure

1. Locate the character or agent in the passage experiencing an emotional trigger or internal state shift.
2. Scan for explicit actions taken by that agent to initiate, inhibit, or modulate that state (pausing, breathing, reframing, delaying response, seeking calm).
3. Verify the action is self-directed and aimed at managing one's own emotion or behavior, not at changing the situation or another person.
4. Check that the modulation occurs in response to a specific emotional stimulus present in the passage.
5. Match the identified behavior against the challenge's question or acceptance criteria before selecting or labeling it.

## Constraints

- Do not flag routine calm behavior that is not preceded by an emotional trigger.
- Do not flag actions aimed at regulating another person's emotions rather than one's own.
- Require both a triggering state and a deliberate modulating action; one alone is not sufficient.
- Do not flag mere suppression that is involuntary or externally coerced.

## Example

Positive: A character feels a surge of anger during an argument, takes a deep breath, and counts to ten before responding. Why: there is a clear trigger and a deliberate self-directed action to modulate the reaction.
Negative: A character speaks calmly throughout a pleasant conversation with a friend. Why: no emotional trigger is present and no modulation is occurring, so surface calmness does not qualify.
