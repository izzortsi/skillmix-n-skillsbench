# folk-physics-common-knowledge-physics

The untrained human perception of basic physical phenomena.

## When to Use

- when the challenge hinges on predicting or interpreting an everyday physical outcome that an average person would infer without formal training

## Procedure

1. Scan the passage and challenge for references to everyday physical events (falling, rolling, floating, breaking, pouring, collisions, temperature changes).
2. For each referenced event, predict the outcome a typical untrained observer would expect based on gravity, inertia, buoyancy, or contact forces.
3. Compare that intuitive prediction against what the passage states or asks; note any gaps the challenge expects you to fill with common-sense physical reasoning.
4. Apply the intuitive prediction only to resolve the specific question posed, avoiding elaboration into formal physics equations or edge cases.
5. Confirm the physical claim involved is one an average person would accept without instruments or calculation before relying on this reasoning.

## Constraints

- Do not invoke this reasoning for scenarios requiring quantitative calculation, specialized equipment, or expert-level physics.
- Do not apply it when the passage explicitly overrides normal physical intuition (e.g., zero-gravity, fictional physics).
- Require that the predicted outcome be one virtually any adult would agree on; uncertain or contested outcomes do not qualify.
- Do not extend intuitive reasoning to microscopic, relativistic, or quantum phenomena where naive expectations fail.

## Example

Positive: The challenge asks what happens to an unsupported cup nudged off a counter. Why: Gravity-driven falling is a universally expected everyday outcome resolvable by untrained intuition.
Negative: The challenge asks the terminal velocity of a feather in a specified air density. Why: It demands quantitative physics beyond common perception, so naive intuition is insufficient.
